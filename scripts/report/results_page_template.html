Part 1

The rules

A plan breaks a rule when any of the conditions below holds. All lengths are in meters. Two rooms closer than m are treated as separated by a wall, not by a gap.

No overlapping rooms

overlap

Rooms may share walls but not floor area. Measured as the summed area of all rooms minus the area they cover together, divided by the area they cover.

breaks the rule if > 2% of the footprint

No holes

holes

Enclosed space that belongs to no room and is wider than a wall. Gaps up to the wall tolerance are closed first, so ordinary walls never count.

breaks the rule if > 2% of the footprint

One connected plan

pieces

Rooms are adjacent when they share at least m of wall. Every room must be reachable from every other room through shared walls.

breaks the rule if the plan falls into > 1 piece

Minimum area

below min. area

Floor area of the room polygon in m². The threshold depends on the room type (table below). For example, a bedroom needs at least 5 m² and a bathroom at least 1.5 m².

breaks the rule if any room's area < its type's minimum

Minimum clear width

below min. width

Diameter of the largest circle that fits entirely inside the room: the widest free spot. A narrow room fails even when its area is large enough. A bedroom needs a 2.0 m circle, roughly a bed plus a passage; a bathroom needs 1.0 m.

breaks the rule if any room's clear width < its type's minimum

Maximum aspect ratio

above max. aspect

Long side divided by short side of the smallest (possibly rotated) rectangle around the room. It catches strip-shaped rooms. Corridors and outdoor areas have no limit.

breaks the rule if any room's ratio > its type's maximum

Thresholds by room type

How often real rooms break them

Share of rooms of each type below the minimum area, below the minimum width, or above the maximum aspect ratio, in the selected dataset.

The thresholds are loose "a person or a bed must fit" defaults, not a building code. These rates show how strict they are in practice.

Part 2

Real datasets compared

Statistics of real plans, plus synthetic ProcTHOR-10K for reference. Entrance and corridor are merged into hallway, because datasets label the same space differently (CubiCasa "Entry Lobby", MagicPlan "Hall", Swiss Dwellings "Corridor"). RPLAN and ResPlan have no hallways; their living room serves as one.

What a plan contains

Mean number of rooms of each type per plan. One plan is one apartment, except CubiCasa5k and MagicPlan (one floor), Swiss Dwellings (one apartment floor) and MSD (a whole building floor with many apartments). CubiCasa5k labels about 20% of its rooms as Undefined, shown as other. LIFULL's "kitchen" usually means a combined living-dining-kitchen.

Room size by type

Area in m² of each room type: the line spans the 5th to 95th percentile, the dot is the median. LIFULL has no metric scale and is left out.

How rooms connect

For each pair of room types: the probability that a room of the row type has at least one neighbour of the column type. "Door" counts interior doors that join two rooms; "shared wall" counts rooms that share at least m of wall. RPLAN and MSD have no interior doors in our copies, so they always show shared walls. The full matrices, including mean connections per plan, are in results/datasets/connections.csv for sampling.

Part 3

Generated plans: which rules break

Published generators with released weights, run on real test inputs. Most methods use one fixed set of 1,000 RPLAN test plans. The exceptions are DiffPlanner (all 12k), DS2D (its own 500-plan subset per room count) and GSDiff unconditional (no input).

Plans breaking at least one rule, RPLAN inputs

Dashed line: real RPLAN plans

  • WallPlan is the only method below the real-data rate (12% vs 17%). GSDiff and iPLAN are close (21–24%).
  • DiffPlanner produces many too-narrow rooms: 8.4% of its rooms are below the minimum width, against 2.7% in real plans.
  • House-GAN++ and MaskPLAN produce too-small rooms (5.8% and 9.7% of rooms below the minimum area, against 0.4% real). MaskPLAN also has too few rooms: 5.1 per plan instead of 6.8.
  • House-GAN's rooms overlap: summed room area exceeds the footprint by 24%, so every plan breaks the overlap rule.
  • DS2D (LLM) overlaps rooms on RPLAN (3–8%), and its 5-room model almost always draws 8 rooms.

Open points

Decisions and next steps

  1. Rule thresholds. Should the minimum areas, widths and aspect ratios follow a specific building norm or region? The per-dataset violation rates above show where the current defaults bite.
  2. Thresholds from data. The size percentiles could set per-dataset thresholds (e.g. the 5th percentile of real bedrooms) instead of fixed defaults.
  3. Request the original RPLAN data to reproduce the official test splits.
  4. Run Floor-plan RLVR (70B), which needs all four GPUs, or skip it.
  • Next: door-based rules (every room reachable from the entrance through doors) for methods that output doors.
  • Next: a pixel-exact converter for raster outputs, so rectilinearity can be reported for every method.
